# Animal Voice: design spec

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: approved (generalized from cats to any animal on 2026-09-15 at the owner's request)

## Goal

An iPhone app that records an animal's vocalizations (cat, dog, bird, any species), lets the owner tag each recording with which animal it was, what the animal was doing, and what the owner thinks the sound meant, and then, once enough labeled sounds exist, tells the owner what a new sound most likely means, with an honest confidence and the past examples that support the guess.

## Decisions already made

| Decision | Choice |
|---|---|
| Platform | Native iOS, Swift, SwiftUI, iOS 17 or later |
| Audience | Public, multi-user product |
| Data storage | On the phone in SwiftData, synced to the user's private iCloud via CloudKit |
| Login | Sign in with Apple for identity; iCloud account for sync |
| Sound analysis | On-device in Swift using Accelerate and AVFoundation |
| Backend | None. No third-party server. |

## Research basis

The Lund University Meowsic project (Schötz, van de Weijer, Eklund) applied phonetic methods to cat vocalizations and reports that meows in positive or requesting contexts tend to be short with a high, rising fundamental frequency (f0), while meows in negative contexts tend to be long with a low, falling f0 ([Schötz et al., "Phonetic Methods in Cat Vocalisation Studies: A report from the Meowsic project", Lund University research portal](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/phonetic-methods-in-cat-vocalisation-studies-a-report-from-the-me/); [Meowsic studies page](https://meowsic.se/studies.html) [project website, not peer-reviewed]). That is why the first-class features are duration, f0 statistics, and f0 contour slope. Spectral shape features (centroid, MFCCs) are added because individual animals differ and per-animal discrimination needs timbre, which is mechanistically plausible rather than proven in the animal-vocalization literature. The Meowsic finding is about cats, so the research hint below is shown only when the animal's species is a cat.

Sample data: `~/Downloads/cat1.m4a`, 91 s, mono, 48 kHz AAC. A first coarse energy scan found 6 loud vocalizations: three meows of 0.36 to 0.44 s with spectral energy peaking near 2.2 to 2.5 kHz, and three short sounds of about 0.1 s with a dominant frequency near 110 to 125 Hz (likely chirps or grunts). Running the exact segmentation algorithm below (with gap bridging and the -50 dBFS floor) yields 12 segments, because six quieter events also fall within 20 dB of the peak. The file is copied into the test fixtures as both the original m4a and a 16 kHz mono WAV made with ffmpeg.

## Architecture

Three Swift modules plus the app target. Each module has one purpose, a small public interface, and its own tests.

```
AnimalVoice.xcodeproj  (generated by XcodeGen from project.yml)
├── Packages/
│   ├── AnimalVoiceAudio/     recording, decoding, segmentation, feature extraction
│   └── AnimalVoiceStats/     feature vectors, classifiers, evaluation, research prior
├── App/                   SwiftUI app: models, views, view models, auth, sync
├── tools/reference/       Python oracle that computes the same features with librosa
└── docs/superpowers/      specs and plans
```

### AnimalVoiceAudio (Swift package, depends only on Accelerate and AVFoundation)

- `AudioLoader.load(url) -> AudioBuffer` decodes any AVFoundation-readable file to mono Float32 at 16 kHz. This caps analyzable pitch at 8 kHz; very high bird calls are out of scope for this version.
- `Segmenter.segment(buffer, config) -> [Segment]` splits audio into vocalization events using frame RMS energy. Frames are 40 ms with 20 ms hop. A frame is active if it is within 20 dB of the buffer's loudest frame and above an absolute floor of -50 dBFS. Runs of active frames shorter than 80 ms are dropped, gaps shorter than 100 ms are bridged.
- `FeatureExtractor.features(buffer, segment) -> VocalFeatures` computes per segment:
  - `durationS`
  - `f0Mean`, `f0Min`, `f0Max` in Hz, and `f0Slope` in Hz/s from a linear fit over voiced frames. Pitch uses the YIN algorithm on 40 ms frames, search range 60 to 3000 Hz, voiced when the YIN aperiodicity is below 0.3. If fewer than 3 voiced frames, f0 fields are nil and `voicedFraction` records why.
  - `rmsDb` (mean loudness), `spectralCentroidHz` (mean over frames)
  - `mfcc` 13 mean coefficients, 26 mel bands, 1024-point FFT, standard librosa layout so the Python oracle can check them.
- `Recorder` wraps AVAudioEngine, records to a `.m4a` in the app's temporary directory, and publishes live RMS for the waveform view. It requests microphone permission and configures the audio session for recording.

### AnimalVoiceStats (pure Swift, no dependencies)

- `FeatureVector` is the fixed-order array of Doubles from `VocalFeatures`. Nil f0 is imputed with the per-animal mean and a `voicedFraction` column carries the missingness.
- `Standardizer` z-scores columns from training data.
- `KNNClassifier` with k = 5, distance-weighted votes, Euclidean on standardized features. Returns a probability over labels and the k neighbor ids.
- `NaiveBayesClassifier` Gaussian per class per feature with variance floor.
- `Ensemble` averages the two probability vectors.
- `Evaluator.leaveOneOut(samples) -> accuracy, per-class recall`.
- `Readiness` rule: predictions are offered only when an animal has at least 2 distinct meaning labels each with at least 5 labeled vocalizations. Below that the app shows "not enough data yet" and how many more are needed.
- `ResearchPrior.hint(species, features) -> String?` returns a one-line hint based on the Meowsic finding above, only when the species is a cat (nil for every other species) ("short, high, rising: often a request or greeting" or "long, low, falling: often distress or complaint"). It is always labeled as a general research prior, never as a prediction for this animal.

### App target

- **SwiftData models** (CloudKit-compatible: every relationship optional with an inverse, no unique attributes, every stored property optional or defaulted, following Apple's stated CloudKit limits ([Apple, "Syncing model data across a person's devices"](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftdata/syncing-model-data-across-a-persons-devices); [Apple, "Creating a Core Data model for CloudKit"](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coredata/creating-a-core-data-model-for-cloudkit))):
  - `Animal`: id, name, species (required text; presets Cat, Dog, Bird, Rabbit, Guinea pig, Horse, or free text), birthdate?, notes, photo (external storage), createdAt
  - `Recording`: id, animal? (set before recording starts), recordedAt, durationS, audio (Data, external storage, m4a), status (`recorded`, `analyzed`, `failed`), notes, activityTag?, meaningTag?, vocalizations
  - `Vocalization`: id, recording?, startS, endS, all `VocalFeatures` fields as stored Doubles, `mfcc` as `[Double]`, activityTag?, meaningTag?, overridden (Bool), userConfidence (1 to 3)?
  - `TagDefinition`: id, kind (`activity` or `meaning`), name, isBuiltIn, sortOrder. Seeded on first launch with species-neutral activities (eating, waiting at door, being petted, playing, alone, greeting a person, looking out window, at the vet, on a walk, other) and meanings (hungry, wants attention, wants out or in, greeting, annoyed, in pain or distress, content, alert or warning, unknown). Users add their own.
- **Sync**: `ModelContainer` with `cloudKitDatabase: .automatic` when the iCloud entitlement is present, `.none` otherwise, so the app runs in the simulator and on a free team without iCloud. Apple's capabilities table lists CloudKit for all membership levels ([Apple, supported capabilities (iOS)](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/reference/supported-capabilities-ios)), but the SwiftData sync page says the iCloud capability requires an active Apple Developer account with admin permissions, so this must be checked against the actual team at build time.
- **Auth**: Sign in with Apple on first launch. Stores the user identifier and display name in the keychain. On each launch, checks credential state and returns to the sign-in screen if revoked. Sign in with Apple identifies the user; CloudKit uses the device's iCloud account for data. The app shows a notice if the two are absent.
- **Screens**:
  1. Sign in
  2. Animals list, add and edit animal (name and species required)
  3. Record: pick which animal first (required, remembered from last time), then big record button, live waveform, elapsed time, stop. On stop, the app runs segmentation and feature extraction in a background task, then opens the tagging sheet.
  4. Tagging sheet: confirm animal, pick activity, meaning, confidence, notes. Applies to every detected vocalization in the recording.
  5. Recording detail: playback, list of vocalizations with start time, duration, f0 mean, and a per-vocalization override of activity and meaning.
  6. History: recordings by animal, filter by activity or meaning.
  7. Ask: record a new sound, show predicted meaning per vocalization with probability bars, the leave-one-out accuracy for this animal, the research-prior hint (cats only), and the 5 nearest labeled past vocalizations with a play button each. A "this was actually…" control saves the correct label and turns the ask into training data.
  8. Insights per animal: label counts, readiness status, accuracy, and a scatter of duration versus f0 mean colored by meaning.

### Data flow

Record → m4a in temp dir → `Recording` row with audio Data → background task: `AudioLoader` → `Segmenter` → `FeatureExtractor` per segment → `Vocalization` rows → status `analyzed`. Tagging writes tags onto the recording and copies them to each vocalization unless overridden. Ask: same pipeline on a new file, then `AnimalVoiceStats` trains on the animal's labeled vocalizations in memory (n is small, training on demand is fine) and predicts.

### Error handling

- Microphone permission denied: explain and deep-link to Settings.
- No vocalization detected: keep the recording, mark it, let the user re-run with a lower threshold slider.
- Feature extraction failure: status `failed`, error kept in notes, recording remains playable.
- iCloud unavailable: app works locally and shows a passive banner.
- Sign in with Apple revoked: sign-in screen, data kept on device.

### Testing

- `AnimalVoiceAudio` tests: synthetic tones (known f0, known duration) must produce f0 within 2 percent and duration within one hop. The sample file `cat1_16k.wav` must yield 12 ± 2 segments with the default config. MFCC and centroid must match a JSON oracle produced by `tools/reference/extract.py` (librosa in a Python 3.13 venv) within tolerances written in the test.
- `AnimalVoiceStats` tests: kNN and naive Bayes on hand-built separable data, leave-one-out on a toy set, readiness rule boundaries, standardizer round trip.
- App tests: SwiftData model round trip in an in-memory container, tag seeding runs once, tag propagation to vocalizations respects `overridden`.
- Build check: `xcodebuild -scheme AnimalVoice -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 16' build test` from the CLI.

## Out of scope for this version

- Cross-user or cross-animal pooled models (for example one model per species). There is no server to pool on and per-animal models are the honest starting point. A future opt-in export could enable this.
- Species-specific analysis parameters. One pitch range (60 to 3000 Hz) and one segmenter config serve all species in this version.
- Background recording, continuous listening, watch app.
- Android or web.
- Deep learning models. The dataset per animal will be tens to hundreds of samples, where kNN and naive Bayes are appropriate.
